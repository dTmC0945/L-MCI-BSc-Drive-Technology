Magnetic Circuits and Materials

The primary objective of these series of lectures is to study devices used in the conversion between electric and mechanical energy. Of course, as this lecture is about electric drives, [1] On paper whereas mostly we will focus more on the machine aspect of drives. emphasis is placed on electromagnetic rotating machinery, by means of which the majority of this energy conversion takes place.

The techniques developed here are generally applicable to a wide range of additional devices including linear machines, actuators, and sensors.

Although NOT an electromechanical-energy-conversion device by strictest definitions, the transformer is an important component of the overall energy-conversion process and is discussed in Chapter ??. The techniques developed for transformer analysis will form the basis for the ensuing discussion of electric machinery. Practically, all transformers and electric machinery use ferromagnetic material [2] A property of certain materials, such as iron, which results in a significant, observable magnetic permeability, and in many cases, a significant magnetic coercivity, allowing the material to become a permanent magnet. Ferromagnetic materials are noticeably attracted to a magnet, which is a consequence of their substantial magnetic permeability [†]Miyazaki, T. & Jin, H. The physics of ferromagnetism (Springer Science & Business Media, 2012). for shaping and directing the magnetic fields which act as the medium for transferring and converting energy, with PMPermanent MagnetAn object made from a material that is magnetized and creates its own persistent magnetic field materials also being widely used. Without these materials, practical implementations of most familiar electro-mechanical energy-conversion devices would NOT be possible.

The ability to analyse and describe systems containing these materials is vital for designing and understanding these devices.
